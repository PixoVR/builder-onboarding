Hazard Recognition

Chapter 2 of Builder 101 · the spec, written before the build

Type: Assessment — spot the hazard Setting: the garage (shared with Tire Change) Slots: 5 Scoring: real-time

The experience

The learner stands in the garage and looks around for things that are unsafe. When they spot something they believe is a hazard, they click it. Catching a real hazard scores a point; clicking something that's actually fine counts as a mistake. When they're done, a board shows how they did — how many hazards they caught, and how many safe things they flagged by accident.

Different every run

There are five fixed spots in the room, and each one can appear either as a hazard or in good condition. Exactly three of the five are hazards each run, chosen at random at the start — so the count is always the same, but which spots are dangerous changes every time and can't be memorized. The same room becomes a fresh test each time it's played.

The five spots

Each spot shows one of two states. Only one is visible per run.

SpotHazard versionSafe version
PalletsLeaning in a messy pileNeatly stacked
Breaker boxPanel left openClosed and labeled
BarrelsCorroded / damagedIn good condition
Fire extinguisherMissing from its mountProperly mounted
DoorwayBoxes stacked, blocking itClear

What a click does

The learner clicks…OutcomeScore effect
a spot that is a hazard this run Correct catch — confirming feedback +1 hazard found
a spot that is safe this run False positive — corrective feedback +1 mistake

Clicking a spot scores it immediately — as a catch or a mistake — and opens a small panel confirming whether it was a hazard or a safe object. The learner closes the panel with OK. Clicking the same spot again reopens the panel, but a spot is only ever scored on the first click.

Keeping score

Three numbers are tracked through the run:

Hazards this runAlways three — the number to find.
Hazards foundCorrect catches.
MistakesSafe spots clicked by accident.

Run flow

  1. Start. Three of the five spots are chosen at random to be hazards; each spot shows its matching version. The score starts at zero. Opening instructions appear on a board in the room.
  2. During. The learner looks around and clicks the spots they think are hazards. Each click gives immediate feedback and updates the score.
  3. End. The learner presses Done. The board reveals the result: hazards found out of three, plus the number of mistakes.
