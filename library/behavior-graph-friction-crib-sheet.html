Behavior Graph Friction — Live Session Crib Sheet

Trainer-facing. For use when a trial/beta participant hits known graph friction. Not shared with participants. Companion to context/ux-overhaul-ideas.md (internal analysis — never excerpt or screenshot it externally).

The three rules

  1. Reactive, not proactive. Acknowledge friction when they hit it. Don't list problems they haven't found.
  2. Acknowledge problems, never name unshipped features. Feature names become remembered commitments. Problem acknowledgments don't.
  3. Make their feedback the visible mechanism. Write their answer down where they can see you do it. Being a design input is the exciting part — use it.

One-time intro line (Session 1 framing, the only proactive mention):

"The Behavior Panel is the most powerful and densest part of the tool. Improving its first-contact experience is an active design focus — and where you stall this week directly ranks that work."

Friction → script, by tier

Tier key: - SIGNAL — safe to imply improvement is coming (small, high-confidence work) - EXPLORE — acknowledge + redirect into a question; no implied timeline - DON'T RAISE — never introduce; response script only if they bring it up

They hit… Say… Tier
Can't find a node; search highlights but doesn't take them there "Known gap — search should jump you to the match. That's the kind of small fix that tends to land early." SIGNAL
Comments/labels too small; wants headings or bigger notes on the graph "Known. Authors want big section headings on the canvas and today's comments can't do that. Expect movement here." SIGNAL
Zoomed out and lost — "what am I looking at?" "You've hit something we've analyzed in depth. We're weighing several directions for making the graph readable at a glance. What would have helped you most right here?" (write it down) EXPLORE
Wiring the same pattern repeatedly (e.g., five near-identical setups) "Real cost, we've measured it in real books. A 'build once, reuse' capability is one of the directions we're weighing. How many times did you copy this one?" (write it down) EXPLORE
Confused by terms — "Datum," "If State is Named," etc. "Fair — that's engineering vocabulary, not author vocabulary. The wording is on our list, separate from how the graph looks. What would you call this?" (write it down) EXPLORE
Tedium placing/arranging nodes by hand "The canvas gives you total layout freedom, and we know that freedom is mostly overhead. On our radar with real analysis behind it." EXPLORE
"Why not blocks, like Scratch?" (only if they raise it) "We've studied that model seriously — it's great for sequences, and most training logic is sequential. There are real trade-offs for the branching cases. It's part of the active exploration, not a committed direction." DON'T RAISE
"Will the graph be replaced?" "The wiring model isn't going anywhere — your books are safe. What we're improving is how readable and navigable it is." DON'T RAISE

When it's a bug, not friction

If the tool did something wrong (silent failure, error, lost work): different script entirely. Don't blend it into UX reassurance. Capture repro steps on the spot, tell them "that's a defect, I'm filing it today," and file it (PLATFORM board, same session). Bugs demand ownership, not framing.

The credibility line (use sparingly, when rigor helps)

"We've traced this behavior to the source code — we know exactly why it happens."

True, and it converts "this tool is rough" into "this team is rigorous." Once per participant, max.

The closer (end of any friction conversation)

"You're one of the first authors outside Pixo to touch this. This exact moment — where you just stalled — is how we rank what gets built next."
